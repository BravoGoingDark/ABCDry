{% trans "The problem" %}

{% trans "Drought arrives quietly. Planting decisions are made blind." %}

{% trans "Northern Tunisia's rain-fed agriculture depends on a short, irregular wet season. When sowing is timed on habit rather than evidence, a dry autumn or a hot spring can cost a whole harvest — and the Ichkeul wetland downstream pays for every litre over-drawn upstream." %}

  1. 01

    {% trans "Erratic rainfall" %}

    {% trans "Rain arrives late, early or all at once. Calendar dates inherited from wetter decades no longer match the season farmers actually get." %}

  2. 02

    {% trans "Rising water demand" %}

    {% trans "Warmer temperatures raise evapotranspiration, so even a normal rainfall year can leave soils in deficit. Rainfall alone hides the real stress." %}

  3. 03

    {% trans "Scattered data" %}

    {% trans "Satellite products, station records and field sensors live in separate systems and formats. Few people on the ground can combine them in time to act." %}

  4. 04

    {% trans "Late warnings" %}

    {% trans "Drought is usually recognised once crops are already stressed. By then, the sowing date and the irrigation plan can no longer be changed." %}

{% trans "The concept" %}

{% trans "One pipeline, from the satellite to the sowing date." %}

{% trans "APWRS measures both sides of the water balance — what falls as rain and what the atmosphere takes back — then turns it into a drought index, a forecast and a concrete planting window for each crop." %}

  1. satellite_alt

    {% trans "Step 1" %}

    {% trans "Observe" %}

    {% trans "CHIRPS rainfall, ERA5 climate reanalysis, MODIS and Sentinel vegetation indices, ground stations and IoT soil-moisture probes." %}

    {% trans "Step 2" %}

    {% trans "Measure" %}

    {% trans "Reference evapotranspiration (FAO-56, Hargreaves) and the water balance P − ET₀, standardised into SPEI from 1 to 24 months." %}
