# How the Drought Risk Score Works (Simple Terms)

This guide explains how the website turns raw data (rain, temperature, soil moisture, satellite images) into a **drought risk score from 0 to 100**.

---

## The Big Picture

Think of the risk score like a **weather forecast for drought**. It answers: *"How dry is it right now, and is it getting worse?"*

```
Raw data (rain, temp, NDVI, soil, SPI)
        ↓
   The Formula
        ↓
Risk score 0-100  →  displayed as:
   • Today's risk
   • Risk in 7 days
   • Risk in 30 days
   • "What's driving the risk?" percentages
   • A written explanation (generated by AI)
```

---

## Step 1 — Gather the last 30 days of data

The system collects the most recent 30 days of records from **6 different tables**:

| Table | What it contains | Example value |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| Soil | Soil moisture (%) | 21% |
| Climate | Rainfall, temperature, wind, humidity | 0 mm today |
| Drought indices | SPI (how dry compared to history) | -1.8 |
| Remote sensing | NDVI (satellite plant health) | 0.28 |
| Hydrology | Streamflow, runoff | low |
| Agriculture | Crop conditions | stressed |

All 6 tables are **merged by date** into one row per day.

> If a day is missing data from one table, the system borrows the nearest value (forward-fill) so nothing is empty.

---

## Step 2 — Calculate the derived values

Using physics formulas, the system computes:

- **ET0 (Potential Evapotranspiration)** — how much water would evaporate from a well-watered field (Penman-Monteith formula, uses temperature, wind, sun, humidity)
- **ETc (Crop Evapotranspiration)** — ET0 × a crop factor (wheat uses more water than olive)
- **Soil water balance** — a daily bank account for the soil: `water in (rain + irrigation) − water out (crop use + runoff) = new soil water`

---

## Step 3 — Read today's numbers

The system pulls the latest values. Example for Bizerte on July 14:

| Value | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Soil moisture | How much water is in the soil right now | 21% |
| Field capacity | Max water the soil can hold | 35% |
| Rainfall (30 days) | Total rain in the last month | 45 mm |
| Rainfall (7 days) | Total rain in the last week | 8 mm |
| Soil trend | Is the soil drying out each day? | -0.55/day |
| NDVI | Satellite health of plants (0.8 = healthy, 0.2 = dying) | 0.28 |
| SPI | How dry vs. normal for this time of year (-3 = extreme drought) | -1.8 |

---

## Step 4 — Calculate the 3 risk components

The system combines three independent signals. Each one is converted to a "penalty score":

### 1. Soil moisture risk (0-100)
How empty is the soil compared to its capacity?

```
soil_risk = (1 − soil_moisture ÷ field_capacity) × 100
          = (1 − 21 ÷ 35) × 100
          = 40
```
- Soil at full capacity → **0** (no risk)
- Soil bone-dry → **100** (extreme risk)

### 2. Vegetation stress penalty (0-20)
Is plant health (NDVI) below the healthy threshold of 0.8?

```
ndvi_penalty = ((0.8 − NDVI) ÷ 0.8) × 20
             = ((0.8 − 0.28) ÷ 0.8) × 20
             = 13
```
- NDVI = 0.8 or more → **0** (plants thriving)
- NDVI = 0 → **20** (plants dead)

### 3. Drought index penalty (0-33)
Is SPI negative (drier than normal)?

```
spi_penalty = max(0, −SPI) × 10
            = max(0, 1.8) × 10
            = 18
```
- SPI = 0 or positive → **0** (normal/wet)
- SPI = -3 → **30** (extreme drought)

---

## Step 5 — Risk TODAY (the main number)

Add all three components together (capped at 100):

```
risk_today = soil_risk + ndvi_penalty + spi_penalty
           = 40 + 13 + 18
           = 71
```

> **71 out of 100** = "High drought risk today"

---

## Step 6 — Forecast the next 7 and 30 days

The system asks: *"If the current trend continues, how bad will it get?"*

First it measures two things:

```
decline_factor   = how fast soil is drying, capped at 2.0
                 = min(2.0, |soil_trend| × 10)
                 = min(2.0, 5.5) = 2.0

rainfall_relief  = how much recent rain helps, capped at 20
                 = min(20, rainfall_7d × 1.5)
                 = min(20, 12) = 12
```

Then it projects forward:

```
risk_7day  = risk_today + (decline × 8)  − (relief × 0.5)
           = 71 + 16 − 6
           = 81

risk_30day = risk_today + (decline × 18) − (relief × 1)
           = 71 + 36 − 12
           = 95
```

**Interpretation:** Soil is drying fast and there's not enough rain to help → risk climbs from **71** to **81** in a week and **95** in a month. This is the trend chart on the analysis page.

---

## Step 7 — "What's Driving the Risk?" (the percentages)

The system gives 5 drivers an absolute score, then shows each as a percentage of the total:

| Driver | How it's scored | Example score |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| Rainfall deficit | Less rain = higher score | 70 |
| Soil moisture decline | Faster drying = higher score | 100 |
| High temperature | Hotter than usual = higher score | 45 |
| Vegetation stress | Lower NDVI = higher score | 72 |
| High evapotranspiration | More water lost = higher score | 60 |

Total = 347 → each divided by 347 × 100:

- Rainfall deficit ≈ 20%
- Soil decline ≈ 29%
- Temperature ≈ 13%
- Vegetation stress ≈ 21%
- Evapotranspiration ≈ 17%

> **So the #1 driver in this example is soil moisture decline** — that's what the bar chart shows.

---

## Step 8 — The AI explanation

The system sends all these numbers to a local AI model (Ollama llama3.2), which writes a human-friendly paragraph like:

> *"Bizerte is facing high drought risk (71/100), driven mainly by rapid soil moisture decline. NDVI indicates vegetation stress, and SPI confirms abnormally dry conditions. Risk is projected to worsen to 95/100 within 30 days if rainfall stays below 8mm/week..."*

---

## When does the Machine Learning kick in?

The website has **two ways** to calculate risk:

1. **Heuristic (always available)** — the formulas above. Works with no training data.
2. **Machine Learning (when models are trained)** — XGBoost and LSTM look at the same 30 days of data and predict risk from patterns they learned.

Currently the website uses the **heuristic** because the ML models were trained on made-up data. To make ML truly useful, it must be trained on **real drought outcomes** (e.g., actual crop losses, or using SPI as the answer key).

## One-line summary of every formula

| What | Formula | Range |
|------|---------|-------|
| Soil risk | `(1 − moisture/field_capacity) × 100` | 0-100 |
| NDVI penalty | `((0.8 − NDVI)/0.8) × 20` | 0-20 |
| SPI penalty | `max(0, −SPI) × 10` | 0-33 |
| **Risk today** | `soil + ndvi + spi` | **0-100** |
| Decline factor | `min(2, \|soil_trend\| × 10)` | 0-2 |
| Rainfall relief | `min(20, rain_7d × 1.5)` | 0-20 |
| Risk 7 days | `risk_today + decline×8 − relief×0.5` | 0-100 |
| Risk 30 days | `risk_today + decline×18 − relief` | 0-100 |
