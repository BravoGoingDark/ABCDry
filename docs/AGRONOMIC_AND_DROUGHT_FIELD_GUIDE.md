# Comprehensive Agronomic & Drought Management Field Guide

This document serves as the authoritative field reference for the APWRS / ABCDry platform, providing scientific and practical guidelines grounded in FAO-56, FAO-66, and Tunisian agro-ecological field research.

---

## 1. Crop-Specific Irrigation & Phenological Water Demand

### 1.1 Durum Wheat (*Triticum durum*) & Barley (*Hordeum vulgare*)
- **Growth Stages & Crop Coefficients ($K_c$):**
  - **Initial / Germination Stage:** $K_c = 0.35$ (0–25 days after sowing). Requires adequate seedbed moisture; irrigation only needed if autumn rainfall is $< 30\text{ mm}$.
  - **Development / Tillering & Stem Elongation:** $K_c = 0.75$ (25–70 days). Water stress causes reduction in spikelet count per head.
  - **Mid-Season / Heading & Flowering (Anthesis):** $K_c = 1.15$ (70–105 days). **Most critical water-sensitive window ($K_y = 1.05$)**. Even mild water stress here results in floret abortion and drastic yield reduction.
  - **Late Season / Grain Filling:** $K_c = 0.85 \to 0.45$ (105–140 days). Water stress causes shriveled grains.
  - **Ripening & Harvest Preparation:** Stop irrigation **15 to 20 days prior to harvest** to allow proper grain desiccation and avoid lodging or fungal blight.
- **Drought Adaptation:** In drought years with SPEI-3 $< -1.0$, prioritize supplementary irrigation at heading/flowering. Barley is more drought-resilient and saline-tolerant than durum wheat, requiring ~25% less water overall.

---

### 1.2 Olive Orchards (*Olea europaea*)
- **Rooting & Water Economy:** Olives have extensive root systems and sclerophyllous leaves with thick cuticles, making them highly drought-tolerant. However, supplemental irrigation dramatically boosts fruit set and oil yield.
- **Regulated Deficit Irrigation (RDI) Strategy:**
  - **Bud Burst & Flowering (March–April):** Keep water stress minimal. Critical for inflorescence development and fruit set. Provide 80–100% of $ET_c$.
  - **Fruit Set (May):** Ensure adequate moisture to prevent premature fruit drop ($K_c = 0.60$).
  - **Pit Hardening (End June to Mid-August):** **The Deficit Window.** Olive trees can tolerate substantial water stress ($K_y = 0.40$). Apply regulated deficit irrigation: cut water supply to **30–50% of $ET_c$** without affecting final oil yield.
  - **Fruit Sizing & Oil Accumulation (Late August to October):** Restore irrigation to 70–80% of $ET_c$. Severe drought here causes small, shriveled drupes with poor oil content.
  - **Post-Harvest / Winter:** Cease irrigation unless winter rainfall is exceptionally low ($< 150\text{ mm}$).

---

### 1.3 Tomatoes & Open-Field Solanaceous Vegetables (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Water Demand:** High seasonal water requirement ($400\text{ to }650\text{ mm}$ depending on planting cycle).
- **Phenological Stages & Sensitivities:**
  - **Establishment:** Frequent light waterings ($K_c = 0.50$).
  - **Vegetative Growth:** $K_c = 0.70\text{–}0.85$.
  - **Flowering & Fruit Setting:** **High Sensitivity ($K_y = 1.10$)**. Water deficit leads to blossom-end rot (calcium deficiency induced by irregular transpiration) and blossom drop.
  - **Fruit Enlargement:** Maintain steady moisture ($K_c = 1.15$). Do not let soil dry completely between irrigations to avoid fruit cracking.
  - **Ripening:** Reduce irrigation frequency to improve fruit brix (sugar content) and firmness.

---

### 1.4 Citrus Orchards (*Citrus sinensis*, *Citrus clementina*)
- **Water Demand:** $700\text{–}950\text{ mm/year}$. Shallow root system requires frequent irrigation cycles.
- **Critical Stages:**
  - **Flowering & Fruit Setting (April–May):** Extremely sensitive. Water stress results in massive flower drop.
  - **Fruit Sizing (June–September):** Water deficit produces small, unmarketable fruit. High summer temperatures with relative humidity $< 30\%$ induce intense atmospheric thirst ($ET_0 > 7\text{ mm/day}$).
  - **Salinity Threshold:** Citrus is sensitive to saline water. Yield drops if electrical conductivity of irrigation water ($EC_w$) exceeds $1.7\text{ dS/m}$.

## 2. Soil Physics & Soil Moisture Management

### 2.1 Available Water Capacity (AWC)
Plant-available water is bounded by **Field Capacity (FC)** and **Permanent Wilting Point (WP)**:
$$\text{AWC (mm)} = \frac{\text{Field Capacity (\%) - Wilting Point (\%)}}{100} \times \text{Root Zone Depth (mm)}$$

- **Sandy Soils:**
  - Field Capacity: $10\text{–}15\%$
  - Wilting Point: $4\text{–}6\%$
  - Available Water: Low ($40\text{–}60\text{ mm/m}$). Requires frequent, small-volume irrigations (micro-drip).
- **Loam / Silt-Loam Soils:**
  - Field Capacity: $22\text{–}28\%$
  - Wilting Point: $9\text{–}12\%$
  - Available Water: High ($120\text{–}160\text{ mm/m}$). Ideal water reservoir for deep-rooted crops.
- **Clay / Vertisols (e.g., Ichkeul plains):**
  - Field Capacity: $32\text{–}42\%$
  - Wilting Point: $16\text{–}22\%$
  - Available Water: Medium-High ($100\text{–}140\text{ mm/m}$). High water holding capacity, but slow infiltration rate ($1\text{–}5\text{ mm/hr}$). Avoid overwatering to prevent soil waterlogging and root hypoxia.

### 2.2 Irrigation Trigger Thresholds (Management Allowed Depletion - MAD)
- Trigger irrigation when soil moisture drops below:
  - **Vegetables & Citrus:** $40\text{–}45\%$ depletion of AWC ($p = 0.40$).
  - **Cereals (Wheat, Barley):** $50\text{–}55\%$ depletion of AWC ($p = 0.50$).
  - **Olives & Almonds:** $65\text{–}70\%$ depletion of AWC ($p = 0.65\text{–}0.70$).

## 3. Drought Indices & Technical Interpretation

### 3.1 SPI vs. SPEI: Understanding the Difference
- **SPI (Standardized Precipitation Index):**
  - Considers precipitation supply only.
  - Ideal for assessing meteorological rainfall deficits.
- **SPEI (Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index):**
  - Considers the climatic water balance: $D = \text{Precipitation} - ET_0$.
  - Takes temperature, solar radiation, wind speed, and humidity into account through $ET_0$.
  - **Why SPEI is the primary index for agriculture:** Under climate warming, even with near-normal rainfall, high summer temperatures increase vapor pressure deficit (VPD) and atmospheric evaporative demand, causing agricultural drought that SPI misses.

### 3.2 Timescales & Drought Types
- **1-Month SPEI/SPI (SPEI-1):** Rapid-onset soil moisture changes; useful for vegetable and shallow-root status.
- **3-Month SPEI/SPI (SPEI-3):** Seasonal crop water balance; primary agricultural drought indicator for cereal planting and seasonal harvests.
- **12-Month SPEI/SPI (SPEI-12):** Hydrological drought; reflects reservoir storage, dam levels, and deep groundwater recharge across regional basins.

## 4. Emergency Drought Mitigation & Water Saving Practices

1. **Night & Early Morning Irrigation:**
   - Irrigating between 22:00 and 06:00 cuts wind drift and evaporative losses by **20% to 35%** compared to midday watering.
2. **Organic Mulching:**
   - Applying a $5\text{–}10\text{ cm}$ layer of organic mulch (cereal straw, chipped pruning wood, olive pomace) on tree basins reduces soil surface evaporation by **30% to 50%**, keeps root zones cooler by $3\text{–}6^\circ\text{C}$, and suppresses weed competition.
3. **Canopy Pruning & Deficit Management:**
   - During severe drought alerts (SPEI-3 $< -1.5$), moderate summer pruning of olive or fruit tree canopies lowers the total leaf surface area (transpirational surface), preventing tree desiccation.
4. **Drip Irrigation Maintenance:**
   - Operating at proper emitter pressure ($1.0\text{–}1.5\text{ bar}$) with regular acid flushing prevents emitter clogging from mineral scale (calcium carbonates common in Tunisian waters).

## 5. Regional Agro-Ecological Zones in Northern Tunisia

### 5.1 Ichkeul Lake & Surrounding Basin
- **Climate Zone:** Sub-humid to humid coastal microclimate; long-term mean annual rainfall $550\text{–}750\text{ mm}$.
- **Soil Types:** Deep alluvial vertisols and clay-loams with high water storage potential.
- **Principal Crops:** Durum wheat, fodder legumes (berseem clover, vetch), artichokes, and olive groves on elevated slopes.
- **Drought Vulnerability:** Wetland buffer buffers short dry spells, but early spring cessation of rains (March–April) requires targeted supplementary irrigation to save cereal grain yields.

### 5.2 Lower Medjerda Valley & Bizerte Agricultural Plains
- **Climate Zone:** Semi-arid to sub-humid transition; mean rainfall $450\text{–}600\text{ mm}$.
- **Agricultural Focus:** Irrigated perimeter (PPI), market vegetables, citrus, table olives.
- **Key Challenges:** Summer water rationing from dams, salinity accumulation in irrigation networks.
